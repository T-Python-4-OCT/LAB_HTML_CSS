The AI Revolution

How artificial intelligence moved from science fiction into our pockets, offices and hospitals — and what comes next.

MA

By Morad Alshehri
October 28, 2026 · 6 min read

Artificial intelligence is no longer a future technology. It writes text, recognizes faces, translates languages, drives cars and helps doctors read scans. Like electricity and the internet before it, AI is becoming a general-purpose technology that touches almost every part of life.

Chapter I

A Short History

The idea of thinking machines is old, but the field of AI officially began in 1956 at the Dartmouth workshop. Early researchers were optimistic, yet progress was slow, and the field went through several "AI winters" when funding dried up.

  1. 1950 Alan Turing proposes the "Imitation Game" to test machine intelligence.
  2. 1997 IBM's Deep Blue defeats world chess champion Garry Kasparov.
  3. 2012 Deep learning wins the ImageNet competition and transforms computer vision.
  4. 2016 AlphaGo beats Lee Sedol at the ancient game of Go.
  5. 2022+ Large language models bring conversational AI to millions of people.

Chapter II

AI Today

Modern AI is powered by three ingredients: huge datasets, powerful hardware (especially GPUs), and better algorithms such as the Transformer architecture. Together they allow models to learn patterns that no human could program by hand.

Chapter V

What Comes Next?

The next decade will likely bring AI assistants that can plan and complete long tasks, robots that work safely beside humans, and scientific discoveries made with AI as a research partner. The real question is not whether AI will change the world, but how we choose to guide it.

For developers and designers alike, the best preparation is simple: master the fundamentals, stay curious, and think critically about the tools we build and use.
